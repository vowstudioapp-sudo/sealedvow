## Problem

PR-46 (11 May 2026, ~23:38 IST) closed part of the trust gap for the authenticated subset of paying customers — they receive a "letter sealed" email with receipt. Subsequent review recognized that PR-46's email itself was incomplete: it confirmed the transaction without delivering the actual artifact (the share link). Three trust gaps remain open:

1. **Guests who pay receive no email.** verify-payment's helper executes Guard 1 (silent skip) when `sessionUser.email` is absent.
2. **Founder-code recipients receive no email at all.** The founder-code branch returns 200 with the share link but never invokes the helper.
3. **Guests have no recovery affordance whatsoever.** A guest who closes their tab after payment loses the letter entirely. Data exists server-side but no UI surface lets them retrieve it.
4. **Authenticated users have an email but it lacks the share link.** A signed-in user who loses the SHARE screen tab has the receipt in their inbox but not the share link. Their recovery affordance is incomplete.

**Underlying gap:** Sealed Vow has no philosophy of continuity for any sender, regardless of auth state. PR-46 closed one symptom (no email for authenticated payers) but missed the deeper question (what should that email contain). PR-46.5 closes the class.

## Current-state findings

Synthesized from the diagnostic (cite for full details):

- **Auth is optional throughout.** `api/create-order.js` never calls `getSessionUser`; `api/verify-payment.js:283-287` resolves session as optional; `PaymentStage` doesn't gate on auth; soft-prompt-with-bypass (`runOrPromptSignIn` + `SignInPromptModal` with `variant === 'payment'`) is the only auth surface in the sender flow.
- **`runOrPromptSignIn` fires on both paid and founder paths.** The same soft-prompt modal is reachable by both populations before reaching the PAYMENT stage. Significant for Q1 resolution.
- **No sender email is captured anywhere.** `CoupleData` in `types.ts:121-228` has no sender email field; form composition doesn't ask; payment doesn't ask; share screen doesn't offer.
- **Dead email infrastructure exists on the landing modal.** `LandingPage.tsx:118-121, 530-531` shows a "Send login link" button wired only to `console.log`. UI-complete, backend-empty.
- **Founder path is structurally separate in fulfillment.** Fulfillment branch at `verify-payment.js:359-412` doesn't call the email helper.
- **No "seal confirmation without receipt" template exists.** `buildLetterSealedEmail` always includes the Amount / Payment ID / Date receipt block.
- **PR-46's email does not include the share link.** Confirmed by inspection of `buildLetterSealedEmail` in `lib/email/sendEmail.js`. Corrected by the unified email architecture below.

## Trust contract

What does Sealed Vow owe every successful sender, regardless of payment method or auth state?

**Minimum trust contract:**

> Every successful seal produces a share link. Sealed Vow attempts operational recovery when sufficient information exists to identify the transaction (paymentId, session email, or guest-provided email). **When email is known, Sealed Vow attempts delivery of the share link to that email**, making the inbox itself the recovery channel. If the sender provided no contact information and loses their tab, recovery is not possible — this is honest, not a failure of the system.

What this contract DOES include:
- Reliable share link generation
- Server-side persistence of share link tied to payment (`payments/{paymentId}` and `shared/{sessionKey}`)
- Best-effort email confirmation including the share link when email is known
- Best-effort operational recoverability via support, when identifying information exists

What this contract does NOT include in V1:
- A guarantee of recovery when no identifying information exists
- Cross-device continuity for guests
- Dashboard access for guests
- SMS, push, or other notification channels
- Deterministic delivery guarantees (all email is best-effort)

## Identity philosophy

**DOCTRINE: Guest email is treated as a delivery destination, not a durable identity primitive.**

Ownership in Sealed Vow remains tied to **Google sign-in only**. A user who signs in with Google gets:
- Durable identity across devices
- "My Letters" dashboard
- All emails captured for that account

A guest who provides an email at the Continue-as-Guest bypass moment gets:
- Best-effort delivery of the seal confirmation (with share link) to that email
- **NO account creation**
- **NO dashboard access**
- **NO cross-device recognition**
- **NO automatic association of past anonymous letters**

### Implications

- No magic-link auth in V1
- No "claim your guest letters" flow in V1
- No email-keyed dashboard in V1
- The dead landing-page "Send login link" placeholder is inconsistent with this doctrine — **Resolved (Q4): delete the placeholder.**

### Rationale

Email = ownership creates a passwordless account system: verification flows, recovery flows, conflict resolution when guest email matches an existing Google account, cross-device session reconstruction. That is a much larger product. PR-46.5 cannot be that product.

Charter non-negotiable 06 (Trust): the product should not lie about what an email field means. The unified email architecture below makes this more important, not less — the email now carries the actual artifact, so its semantics must be unambiguously "delivery channel," not "account-bound asset locker."

## Guest continuity philosophy

Continuity is offered to authenticated users by default and to guests by opt-in. For guests, V1 continuity has three layers:

1. **In-tab continuity.** A guest mid-composition can refresh and resume; `vday_data_draft` in localStorage handles this. (Current state.)
2. **In-session continuity.** A guest who completes payment receives the share link on the SHARE screen. (Current state.)
3. **Cross-session continuity (opt-in, NEW).** A guest who provided email at the bypass moment receives best-effort delivery of the share link by email. If they lose the tab AND the email arrives, the email is the recovery channel. If the email fails to arrive (Resend failure, inbox rejection, network failure), there is no further recovery beyond support intervention.

What is NOT offered to guests:
- Cross-device continuity beyond email recovery
- Re-claim of anonymous letters via email match
- Dashboard view

What IS offered to all senders by Sealed Vow operations:
- Support recovery via paymentId or email lookup, **when identifying information exists**. Process, not product feature.

## Notification philosophy

**Core principle: The product does not lie about what an email field means.**

Every design choice in PR-46.5 — copy, surface placement, post-success messaging — must pass this test: "If a careful user read this and asked 'what does providing my email here commit me to,' could we give an answer that matches what the system actually does?" If no, redesign.

### Unified email architecture

Sealed Vow sends one kind of email after a successful seal: the **seal confirmation**. It always contains the share link. It conditionally contains a receipt block.

| Sender path | Email contains share link? | Email contains receipt block? |
|---|---|---|
| Razorpay capture + authenticated sender | Yes | Yes (Amount, Payment ID, Date) |
| Razorpay capture + guest with email | Yes | No |
| Founder-code + email captured at bypass | Yes | No |
| Any path, no email known | (No email sent) | (No email sent) |

**Shared rendering architecture, one mental model.** The receipt block is a conditional include in the rendering pipeline, not a parallel template. This intentionally avoids constraining implementation to a single HTML blob — composable blocks within a unified rendering pipeline are the correct shape.

This unifies what earlier iterations had split into three parallel templates. The split was philosophically correct on email ≠ identity but architecturally incomplete on email content. The unified pattern: **email = artifact delivery, for everyone we have email for**.

### Implementation copy constraints (philosophy-level, not template-level)

Copy in the email and at the email-capture surface must satisfy the truthfulness principle. Examples of language that fails:
- "Access your letter here" — implies account/recovery semantics
- "Your dashboard awaits" — implies dashboard ownership
- "Get updates from Sealed Vow" — implies marketing consent

Examples of language that passes:
- "Your sealed letter is ready to be shared: [link]" — true to product purpose
- "Your share link: [link]" — minimal, accurate
- "We'll send the share link here. No account is created." — explicit at capture surface

These are constraints, not prescriptions. Final copy is implementation-level.

### Principles

- All notifications are **best-effort**. Helper never throws, never blocks fulfillment.
- Email delivery is **never promised optimistically** in UI. SHARE screen always shows the share link; email is mentioned only if delivery succeeded (Q3 resolved).
- Email content is **Charter-compliant**: editorial restraint, ceremonial register, no marketing voice, no growth CTAs.

## Non-goals

PR-46.5 explicitly does NOT include:

- Guest accounts of any form
- Magic-link or passwordless auth
- Cross-device guest continuity
- Dashboard for guests
- Re-claim of anonymous letters via email match
- Receiver-side email (e.g., notify receiver when they open the letter)
- SMS notifications
- Reminder, anniversary, or any non-immediate emails
- Automated refunds (that's PR-48)
- Founder-code recipient identification at the influencer's end
- Email lifecycle management beyond transactional storage

If any of these are discovered to be necessary during implementation, **stop and escalate to a design conversation.**

## Risks

1. **Identity confusion risk.** Guests who provide email may infer they have an account. Mitigation: copy at the email-field surface and within the email itself must be explicit per Notification philosophy core principle.
2. **Email lifecycle risk.** Stored guest emails create low-stakes data accumulation. Retention policy (Q2 resolved): store on `notifications/{paymentId}` alongside `letterSealedSentAt` and `resendMessageId`, with new field `recipientEmail` (or similar). No separate `guests/` collection.
3. **Founder-code email-capture asymmetry risk.** Resolved (Q1): founder recipients use the same surface as paid guests.
4. **Delivery reliability risk.** Resend can fail. Current helper records `resendMessageId` on delivery; no retry. Acceptable V1 if Resend success >95%. Retry logic is a future PR.
5. **Charter risk: email field as nudge.** See Notification philosophy core principle. The bypass-moment email field must pass the truthfulness test.
6. **Over-promising recovery risk.** Mitigated in weakened trust contract and Q3 resolution. UI never claims email delivery without `resendMessageId` confirmation.
7. **Scope creep risk.** Re-read Non-goals before any implementation conversation.
8. **Email-forwarding privacy risk.** Signed-in users may forward the receipt email to accountants for reimbursement. The share link goes with it. Mitigation: visually separate the receipt block from the share-link block in the email template so the user can screenshot or copy the receipt portion alone. Doesn't fully eliminate the risk — a determined forwarder still exposes the link — but reduces accidental exposure to near zero.
9. **PR-46 email modification risk.** PR-46.5 modifies the production-validated PR-46 email template (adding share link, restructuring layout). This is a small change to a recently-shipped artifact. Risk class: low (template change is deterministic and idempotent) but worth flagging. Implementation should re-run PR-46's verification checklist after the template change.

## Resolved decisions

### Q1 — Founder-code email-capture surface

**Decision: Option (d) — Founder recipients inherit the exact same guest continuity surface as paid guests.**

- Same `SignInPromptModal` (variant === 'payment') already fires in the founder flow via `runOrPromptSignIn`
- Same optional email field beneath the Continue-as-Guest button
- Same seal-confirmation email
- Same delivery semantics
- Same identity philosophy

**Rationale:** Symmetric architecture, single mental model, no parallel-product anti-pattern. Asymmetric continuity for founder recipients would create the exact pattern PostLaunch_Architecture_Roadmap_v2 explicitly works to avoid.

### Q2 — Email retention semantics

**Decision: Store guest email on `notifications/{paymentId}`, NOT on `payments/{paymentId}`.**

**Rationale:**
- `payments/` is the financial ledger. Mixing identity-adjacent data into payment records invites future engineers to treat email as an identity primitive.
- `notifications/` is the communication artifacts store. Email-as-delivery-destination belongs here philosophically.
- Future deletion / GDPR evolution is cleaner with the separation.

**Implementation implication:** Extend `notifications/{paymentId}` schema to include `recipientEmail` (or similar field name) alongside existing `letterSealedSentAt` and `resendMessageId`.

### Q3 — SHARE-screen email-delivery messaging

**Decision:**
- **Helper success** (`resendMessageId` captured): SHARE screen shows a quiet line — "We also sent your share link to your email." No bold, no callout, no emphasis.
- **Helper failure** (no `resendMessageId`): SHARE screen says nothing about email. The share link itself is the primary affordance and is shown prominently as always.
- **No retry CTAs.** No "check your spam." No "resend email" button. The share link is the artifact; email is secondary.

**Rationale:** The truthfulness principle constrains this directly. Adding retry CTAs would imply email delivery is critical recovery — but the share link on SHARE screen is the primary recovery affordance. "Check your spam" framing is anti-Charter.

### Q4 — Dead landing-page email-link

**Decision: Delete the placeholder.**

- One-line change
- Eliminates philosophical contradiction with Identity philosophy
- Include in PR-46.5 scope

### Q-new — Unified email architecture (share link in all emails)

**Decision: Every confirmation email contains the share link. Receipt block is a conditional include for paid + authenticated cases. Shared rendering architecture, one mental model.**

**Rationale:**
- The share link is the actual trust artifact; the receipt is metadata. The product fails emotionally if the user loses the share link; payment IDs and amounts are recoverable through other channels.
- Asymmetry where signed-in users get less continuity (receipt only) than guests (share link only) is incoherent.
- Self-service recovery via inbox extends to all populations with email, reducing operational support burden and aligning PR-48 (auto-refund) to focus on true fulfillment failure.

**Implementation implication:** PR-46.5 modifies the email rendering pipeline (likely `buildLetterSealedEmail` and/or a new unified function) so that the share-link section is always rendered and the receipt block is conditionally included. PR-46's existing call site keeps working without conditional logic — the conditional lives inside the rendering pipeline, not the caller.

## Implementation scoping considerations (NOT doctrine — surfaced for the next session)

These are noted here for traceability but do not belong in the doctrine layer. Address during implementation scoping:

- **Link-preview risk.** Some messaging apps and email clients prefetch URLs for previews. Verify the receiver share link is safe to prefetch — confirm it doesn't trigger receiver analytics, open-state mutations, or accidentally consume one-time tokens. Likely already safe but worth confirming.
- **Subtle future risk (awareness, not action).** Two parallel recovery systems are forming: auth users have dashboard + inbox + support; guests have inbox + support only. This is fine in V1. Later, operational support burden may push toward "just let guests reclaim letters via email." That pressure is where doctrine erosion will happen. The Identity philosophy doctrine specifically protects against this convenience-driven decay.
